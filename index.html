About Us

Mission
Statement

To preserve, revive, and promote Kathak as a living, evolving art form in Pakistan by upholding its classical integrity, nurturing new generations of dancers through rigorous training, and making the art accessible beyond elite spaces. Through performance, education, and outreach, Mohsin Babar is committed to restoring the dignity of Kathak, destigmatizing the ghungroo as the dancer's instrument, and ensuring that this rich storytelling tradition continues to inspire, educate, and belong to all.

Our
Philosophy

At Mohsin Babar Productions, we believe that art must belong to everyone. Kathak, once a storytelling tradition rooted in the people, cannot survive if confined to elite spaces. Our philosophy is grounded in inclusion—opening our doors through scholarships to students who would otherwise never access classical training, and taking our performances beyond urban centres into small towns and rural communities where art is rarely experienced in its classical form. We are committed to nurturing talent wherever it exists and sharing Kathak with audiences wherever they are, because an art form does not live by being preserved in isolation, but by being practiced, seen, and shared widely.

Our
Founder

Mohsin Babar is an internationally acclaimed choreographer, dancer, and performing artist, specializing in Kathak, Pakistani folk dance, Sufi dance, and contemporary/modern dance. With over 27 years of experience, he has performed across the world, including in the United States, United Kingdom, Sweden, China, Japan, India, Brunei, Malaysia, Singapore, and many other countries.

Mr. Babar is regarded as one of Pakistan's most highly trained Kathakars. He received rigorous training under two of the country's pioneering Kathak masters, Ustad Hamid Hussain Shaad Jaipuri and Ustad Shado Maharaj. He also underwent advanced training for three years under Ustad Rajendra Gangani at the National School of Drama in India.

He is the founder of Mohsin Babar Productions and the Kathak School of Performing Arts—the only institute in Karachi dedicated exclusively to teaching Kathak.

Our
Team

Our team is a collective of dedicated dancers and artists, trained in the discipline of Kathak and contemporary movement. Many come from underprivileged backgrounds and are supported through full scholarships, reflecting our commitment to inclusion, excellence, and cultural preservation.

Our
Patron

Naween A. Mangi is a rural development specialist, social entrepreneur, media professional, writer, and patron of dance-theatre as a form of cultural preservation. She is the Founder and Managing Trustee of the Ali Hasan Mangi Memorial Trust (AHMMT), an integrated rural development organization that has worked across over 500 villages in Sindh on education, healthcare, infrastructure development, and emergency response.

Educated at Karachi Grammar School, she went on to earn a Bachelor's degree in Economics from the London School of Economics, and later a Master's degree in Journalism from New York University, specializing in Business and Economic Reporting.

Naween began her career as a financial journalist, contributing to publications including The Friday Times, Herald Magazine, and Asia Times. She served as Pakistan Bureau Chief for Bloomberg News from 2006 to 2015, establishing the agency's first bureau in Pakistan and significantly expanding international visibility of the country's economic landscape.

Since 2015, she has focused on philanthropy, cultural work, and writing. She contributes regularly to Dawn, teaches journalism courses at the Institute of Business Administration (IBA) Centre for Excellence in Journalism, and continues to lead AHMMT's development work across Sindh — work that earned the trust an award from the University of California, Berkeley in 2018.

Alongside her development and media work, Naween is a committed patron and producer of dance-theatre, supporting it as a vital form of cultural preservation and social dialogue. Through collaborations with artists and institutions, she has helped bring classical and contemporary dance-theatre to new audiences — including community-based performances that address social issues and reclaim dance as a serious artistic and cultural practice.

Productions

Mohsin Babar
Productions

We tell stories through movement. From ballet-style dance theatre to short thematic dance films, our work explores the human experience—devotion, identity, and transformation. Rooted in Kathak, and drawing from Sufi, contemporary, and folk traditions, we create performances that speak to the present while carrying the depth of tradition. Alongside this, we develop community theatre that engages with urgent social themes, taking art beyond the stage and into the lives of people.

Portfolio

Our Projects

Project 01

Khwaab

A dance-theatre production exploring the essential human experience—of love, loss, longing, and transformation—told entirely through movement. Written, choreographed, and directed by Mohsin Babar, Khwaab brings together Kathak, contemporary, Sufi, and folk dance forms in a layered narrative that is both deeply personal and universally resonant.

Project 02

Soch Badlen, Zindagi Sawarein

A community theatre production developed to spark dialogue around family planning and the realities of everyday life in rural Sindh. Performed across multiple villages, it brings theatre directly into communities, using storytelling as a tool for awareness and reflection.

Project 03

In C

A contemporary dance work reinterpreted in Pakistan by Mohsin Babar Productions, supported by Goethe-Institut in Karachi. Performed at the Karachi Arts Council World Festival 2025, the piece brought an international vocabulary of movement into dialogue with a local ensemble of dancers.

Project 04

Aqsaam-e-Naach

A curated dance production exploring the many dimensions of Kathak—from traditional repertoire to contemporary and fusion pieces—revealing the depth, versatility, and storytelling power of the form. A statement of purpose: to restore Kathak's place in our cultural landscape.
